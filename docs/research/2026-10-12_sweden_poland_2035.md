# Sweden and Poland in 2035: the evidenced tight cases, and the three neighbours tight together

Branch `research/sweden-poland-2035`, from 8754925 (the research on Finland). Opened 2026-10-12.
Everything here that would move a figure in the app is **Proposed**: nothing in the app changes.

## §0 The question, the rules and the acceptance (written before any run)

**The request** (the owner, 2026-10-12, translated from Russian): "Let's carry out the same research for
Sweden and Poland, so that the forecast is also high-priced, like Finland's. So that later we assemble
them into a separate profile that sets high prices for all three neighbours at once."

**What the research answers.**
- The request names a direction, and the research does not take it as given.
- For each country, the research builds the tight case the evidence supports: demand comes, the build
  lags.
  - Every assumption is sourced, with the counter-evidence beside it.
  - The research reports what has to be true for the price that case gives.
- Its result reads "the neighbours tight, conditional on …". It is never "the forecast".

**Decided with the owner (2026-10-12)**
- **"High"** means the evidenced tight case, built like Finland's case C. It is reported against:
  - the profile in force (SE4 60.2 and PL 78.6 €/MWh);
  - the evidence band;
  - the record and Estonia;
  - a line per country, showing which conditions give which level.
- **Germany**, which sets SE4's and Poland's price in their tight hours, is priced by the method in
  force: TYNDP DE 2035, re-priced to the scenario's fuels, with the record's shape. A variant puts it
  at SvK LMA 2026's level.
- **Carried over from the research on Finland:**
  - the day-ahead price only;
  - a zone model on v5's engine, tested on the record before any use.

**Rules**
1. **Fixed before the runs.**
   - This §0 is committed before any test, and the case table (§3) before any 2035 run.
   - Later changes go only into sections marked "added after the runs", and the first-run tables
     stay.
2. **Reported beside each case:**
   - the TSOs' own views: SvK LMA 2026, PSE's PRSP 2027–36, and TGE's BASE Y-30;
   - the decomposition: in force → case 0 (the method) → A → C alone → C coupled;
   - the share of hours at the peakers' and flexible loads' bids;
   - CO₂'s share of the marginal cost.

   Only the three climate years weighted together are reported, never one alone.
3. **v5 stays as it is.**
   - The models live in `spikes/neighbours-2035/`; Finland's in `spikes/finland/`.
   - The data scope stays D-002's. The Swedish and Polish series are read from v4's cache, read-only,
     each file checked against its v4 lineage record's sha256.
   - A missing month is an error, except these listed gaps:
     - Swedish A31 after the flow-based go-live;
     - SE4–DE's A31 from 2024-07 to the go-live;
     - SE2–NO4, which is not in the cache.
   - No network data is fetched. Published documents are registered and cached by the pipeline's
     document fetcher (R-DATA-7).
4. **Sources are registered before they are read:**
   - `pipeline/sources/documents.py`;
   - `data/documentary/parameters.csv` (rows `se2035.*`, `pl2035.*`).
5. **Fuels.**
   - In 2035, the neighbours run on the scenario's fuels:
     - the knots of `repriceKnots` for the TYNDP-based neighbours;
     - the ERAA 2025 rows of the fuel path for Poland's thermal plant.
   - The record years run on the record's fuels.
6. **Proposed until the owner decides.** `make ci` is green before each commit, and nothing is pushed.

**The models (§2)**
- **Sweden: a chain SE12 – SE3 – SE4,** with the engine's links as cuts 2 and 4.
  - SE1 and SE2 are merged. SE12's price is judged against SE2's, with SE1's reported beside it.
  - **Reservoir:** SE12's hydro, released per week between the week's 10th- and 90th-percentile hours.
    - Record: the week's observed energy.
    - 2035: TYNDP DE 2035's own SE01+SE02 hydro output for that week and climate year. Swedish inflow is
      snowmelt, so it cannot serve as a weekly budget.
  - **Given hourly:** SE3's and SE4's hydro and SE3's nuclear.
  - **Thermal** (A75 "other", the CHP fleet) uses Finland's method:
    - the week's 10th-percentile hour as heat-bound output;
    - above it, a ladder fitted on the record.
  - **Wind and solar** are offered at 0.
  - **Neighbours** are price-takers at their A44 price, Poland at its R-BND-3 boundary series.
- **Poland: one zone.**
  - **Thermal plant by fuel class**, each class offering its week's maximum above its week's
    10th-percentile hour (must-run):
    - hard coal and lignite in three vintages, split by PEMMDB's 2030 class shares;
    - CCGT and OCGT, and the rest.
  - **Bids** are the monthly short-run cost plus one additive markup fitted on the record:
    - efficiencies, CO₂ factors and VOM from ERAA 2025's common data;
    - fuels: TTF, PSCMI 1 at the ECB's EUR/PLN, the monthly EUA, and lignite at ERAA 2025's
      brown-coal price.
  - **Pumped storage** is a store.
  - **The five borders** are given as observed net flows.
- **The records' balance** is generation + net imports − load, with pumped storage's own output left
  out because the model dispatches it. A month off by more than 100 MW gets the difference:
  - a shortfall as given output, shaped like the month's thermal output;
  - an excess as load, shaped like the load.

**Test 2, the record (as R-VAL-1).** Per zone and period, model against record:
- the mean within ±10 %;
- P5, P50 and P95 within ±15 %, or ±5 €/MWh where the value is under 33;
- the hours at or below 5 and at or above 150 €/MWh within ±25 %;
- Spearman ≥ 0.85.

Sweden's borders are judged separately. The HVDC borders (SE3–FI, SE3–DK1, SE4–DE, SE4–PL, SE4–LT) are
judged on two counts:
- the share of import-limited hours within ±10 points;
- the net flow within ±20 % of A11.

The AC borders (SE1–FI, SE1–NO4, SE2–NO3, SE3–NO1, SE4–DK2) and the cuts (SE2–SE3, SE3–SE4) are judged
on net flow only. An AC border's physical flow is not its commercial exchange (Finland, §2a). Poland is
judged on price alone, because its borders are given: the allocation constraint caps its total net
position.

- **Fitting and blind periods:**
  - **Sweden.** The ladder and the reservoir's horizon (`DAY_AHEAD` or `WEEKLY`) are fitted on 2023.
    - The binding blind test is 2024-01-01 to 10-28, the capacity era.
    - 2025 runs under flow-based: its capacities are JAO's non-simultaneous maxima. It is reported as
      run, and with each zone's error removed quantile by quantile, the correction of Finland's §2a,
      written here in advance.
  - **Poland.** The markup is fitted on 2023–2024 by the mean absolute error, as D-118. 2025 is blind
    and binding.
- **Stop rule.** The binding criteria are the mean, P50 and Spearman on the blind period.
  - A country failing one of them is not used for 2035, and the failure is reported.
  - The tails (P5, P95, hours ≤ 5 and ≥ 150) and the borders are reported. Where the tails fail, the
    quantile shift is shown beside.

**Test 1, case 0 (TYNDP's own Sweden and Poland).** The model gets TYNDP DE 2035's inputs per climate
year:
- demand, with electrolysis at the re-pricing's steps;
- the fleet and the hydro output;
- the neighbours' marginal cost.

Its prices must meet TYNDP's marginal cost:
- the mean within ±10 %;
- Spearman ≥ 0.8;
- the net exchange within ±10 %.

A failure is reported with its reason and does not stop the research; for Finland the reason was
hydrogen. Case 0(b) is the same run at the scenario's fuels, set against the level in force.

**The 2035 cases (§3), written before the runs**
- **Cases:** 0; A, the TSOs' central; C, tight; and C with Germany at LMA 2026's level.
- **Adequacy.** Peakers at the OCGT short-run cost of the scenario's fuels are added by bisection on a
  250 MW grid, until the country meets its own sourced reliability standard.
  - Fingrid's 30 % flexible-load rule is not transplanted.
  - A country needing more than 20 GW of peakers is reported infeasible, not rescued.
- **The line:** one 3 × 3 grid per country, standalone.

**The coupled runs (§5)**
- Sweden and Poland solve as one chain [SE12, SE3, SE4, PL], with SwePol as its third link.
- Finland's zone takes SE12's and SE3's prices and EstLink's flow; it runs the Finland research's case A
  or C.
- The chain takes the SE–FI, NordBalt and LitPol flows; v5 takes the FI, SE4 and PL prices.
- Flows are damped by half a step. The run stops when the FI, SE4, PL and EE means each move by less
  than 0.5 €/MWh, within 8 rounds. A run still moving by more than 2 €/MWh after 8 rounds is not used.
- **Runs:** all-A and all-C, on v5's four scenarios.
  - The all-C series is also held fixed, as an app profile would hold it. The difference from the
    coupled run is that profile's error.

## §1 The evidence, both ways (read 2026-10-12)

Register keys are those of `pipeline/sources/documents.py`; pages are the PDF's.

**Sweden**

*For a tight Sweden*
- **Wind has stalled.** 1.0 GW was added in 2024 and 1.4 GW in 2025, but only 0.74 / 0.23 / 0.19 GW are
  expected for 2026–28, reaching 19.6 GW by end-2028. Turbine orders in 2025 were under 0.03 GW
  (`gps_q1_2026` p.5).
- **No offshore wind.** 13 Baltic projects were rejected in November 2024 and 11 more on 16 Jul 2026, on
  defence grounds. Vattenfall does not pursue Vidar, Kriegers Flak is paused, and there are no FIDs and
  no support scheme (`gov_offshore_2026`). LMA has no offshore before 2040 except in HF
  (`svk_lma_2026` p.151).
- **No new nuclear before 2035** (`svk_lma_2026` p.156).
- **Cables lost or postponed.** On 7 May 2026 the government took Aurora Line 2, Fenno-Skan 3 and
  Konti-Skan Connect out of SvK's plan for 2027–29 (`gov_invplan_2026`).
  - Konti-Skan 1/2 reach end of life in the mid-2030s, and Fenno-Skan 1 already runs reduced
    (`svk_invplan_2026` pp.2–4).
  - LMA's end-of-life test has Baltic Cable's last year in 2033 (`svk_lma_2026` Tab 13 p.130).
  - Hansa PowerBridge was dropped (2024).
- **Cut 2's timing.** It is 9 600 MW in 2034 and 10 350 MW only in 2036 (`svk_lma_2026` Tab 10 p.55).
  The NordSyd lines arrive 2030–35 (`svk_nup_2026` pp.35–36).
- **Large loads.** Data centres reach 9 / 27 / 40 / 41 TWh in 2040 (LF/MM/HP/HF) and hydrogen
  9 / 27 / 41 / 41 TWh (`svk_lma_2026` Fig 5 p.42, Tab 4 p.43). SE1's steel hydrogen counts as
  inflexible (p.58).
- **Batteries.** LMA assumes 5.5 GW of large batteries by 2030, against 1.6 GW installed and 0.4 GW
  under construction (`svk_lma_2026` p.165; `gps_q1_2026` p.2).
- **Peakers.** Karlshamn 2 (335 MW) is mothballed. SE4's 440 MW CCGT is kept for preparedness only
  until 2029.
- **Adequacy.** ERAA 2025 puts LOLE in 2035 at 4.4 h in SE3 and 6.6 h in SE4 (`acer_eraa_2025` Tab 1 p.3).
  SvK finds more than 2 GW needed in southern Sweden to meet a 1 h standard (`svk_leveranssaker_2026`
  Tab 2 p.18).

*Against*
- **Demand keeps being revised down.** KMA 2025 is 5–12 TWh a year below KMA 2024, and LMA 2026 is
  about 50 TWh below LMA 2024 in 2030 (`svk_kma_2025` p.9; `svk_lma_2026` pp.145–146).
- **Use was 134 TWh in 2025.**
- **Every Energimyndigheten scenario keeps Sweden a net exporter of 24–51 TWh in 2035.**
- **Projects are failing.** Stegra needs new funding after an overrun of SEK 10 bn or more, and its CEO
  was replaced on 30 Sep 2026. Northvolt is bankrupt, FlagshipONE cancelled, and LKAB's Kiruna DRI has
  moved to the 2040s.
- **ERAA's tightness rests on SvK's old high-demand scenario** (about 269 TWh in 2035). Before
  curtailment sharing, LOLE is 0.9 h in SE3 and 0.6 h in SE4 (`acer_eraa_2025` Tab 7 p.16). LMA finds
  LOLE under 1 h everywhere in 2040 (`svk_lma_2026` pp.104–106).
- **LMA 2026 puts SE4 below Germany in every scenario:** 61.1–74.5 against 73.6–80.9 €/MWh in 2040
  (Fig 26 p.75).

*The reliability standard:* LOLE 1 h a year, set by the government on 17 Nov 2022 (`gov_rs_2022`). Ei
reviews it in 2026.

**Poland**

*For a tight Poland*
- **Coal capacity payments end.** The 550 g CO₂ derogation ends on 31 Dec 2028 and has no legal basis
  beyond it (`pse_prsp_2027_2036_apr2026` p.12). About 14.5 GW of coal loses capacity payments from
  2029.
- **The new coal units' contracts end in 2035**, and many units are planned to close at end-2035
  (ERAA 2025 Annex 6, p.35).
- **PSE's own adequacy for 2035, in its three scenarios.** Mean LOLE is 30 / 107 / 405 h, needing
  2.6 / 4.8 / 8.0 GW more firm capacity to meet 3 h (Tabs 10.5–10.8 pp.100–102). This excludes imports,
  demand response and nuclear.
- **Gas slips.** Every contracted gas plant has moved by 1–3 years. Połaniec found no bidder and moved
  to 2035 (press, 2026).
- **Onshore wind is held back.** 11.0 GW in June 2026, with the 700 m rule still in force after the
  2025 veto.
- **The first nuclear unit comes at end-2036** (PRSP Tab 4.7 p.39).
- **The record is high.** The 2025 average was 104.3 €/MWh, dearer than DE, CZ and LT. September 2026
  averaged about 152.

*Against*
- **The government's plan passes PSE's own check.** KPEiK WAM (adopted 8 Jun 2026) meets the 3 h
  standard. It relies on about 11 GW of batteries and 8.5 GW of gas already won in capacity auctions
  (`kpeik_2026_annex1_wam` p.75).
- **In PSE's 2036 market run, Poland is a net exporter** and curtails 19 TWh of renewables (PRSP Tab 5.1
  p.51).
- **Demand has been flat for a decade** (+0.96 % a year). Heat pumps, EVs and data centres run far below
  PSE's paths, and the government puts data centres at 6–8 TWh.
- **ACER says PSE understates imports** (`acer_opinion_01_2025`).
- **The deputy minister** says "the capacity deficit is smaller than we thought" (August 2026).
- **TGE's forwards fall** to 100.3 €/MWh for 2030 (BASE Y-30, 9 Oct 2026).

*The reliability standard:* LOLE 3 h a year, Dz.U. 2024 poz. 1389 § 3 (`mkis_reg_2024_1389`).

### §2b Added after the first run

**Two mechanisms of v5's own, fitted on the fit years alone and judged on the same blind periods.**
- **R-BND-6's slope, one value for every Swedish border.** A grid of 0, 2, 5, 10, 20, 40 and 80 €/MWh per
  GW on 2023 kept 0: every slope raised the MAE, from 5.75 to 17.68. Sweden's results are unchanged.
- **R-SUP-6's utilisation bins for Poland** (0 / 50 / 75 / 90 / 100 % of each offer's weekly maximum).
  Four markups were fitted on 2023–2024 by the MAE, by coordinate descent over 43 candidates: −10 / 0 /
  10 / 20 €/MWh.
  - 2025 blind: mean 86.6 against 104.3, P50 92.8 against 104.5, Spearman 0.807. **Fail.**
  - The hours ≥ 150 were 41 against 1 010: the MAE does not reward the tail.

**Two more, each a method this research already uses, defined here before their run:**
- **Sweden: the shift 2023 teaches.** For each zone, the record's value minus the model's at each rank of
  2023 (`rankShift`, as `fbShift`) is applied to the 2024 run and judged on its capacity-era hours.
  - SE3's and SE4's hourly order already passes; their level and middle sit high, which a shift by rank
    corrects without touching the order.
  - In 2035 the same shift, learned on 2023, would be applied to the Swedish series and shown beside the
    unshifted ones.
- **Poland: Finland's ladder per fuel class.** It is read on 2023–2024 against the price's premium over the
  class's reference vintage (hard coal old 2, lignite old 2, CCGT present 1).
  - The premium's edges are −40 / −20 / 0 / 20 / 40 / 80 / 150 €/MWh, with the share below them at −60 and
    the rest at +300.
  - The bids move with the fuels, so the ladder carries to 2035's. The vintages' merit order gives way to
    the record's.
- **The same binding criteria apply, once.** If either fails, that country's zone model is not used.
  Its 2035 level then comes from the evidence alone (§3), as a case without a model.

**What the evidence and the models say**

1. **On the TSOs' own central paths (A), the three neighbours are cheaper than the profile in force.**

**The averaged iteration (a change of §0's coupling rule).** §0 damped the flows by half and stopped at

average of the rounds' responses — the flow of round k moves 1/(k+1) of the way to the new response —
and stop when the averages move by less than 0.5 €/MWh, within 20 rounds (`NB_ROUNDS`). §0's limit
stands: a run that still moves more than 2 €/MWh is not used.
