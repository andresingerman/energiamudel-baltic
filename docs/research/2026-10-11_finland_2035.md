# Finland dearer than Estonia by 2035? (2026-10-11)

The owner's request: "do further research, taking into account that tenders for offshore wind farms are failing, google will build data centers in Finland … plus 7 TWh … our forecast is that in Finland by 2035 electricity will be more expensive than in Estonia, we need to gather the evidence and model such a scenario."

The owner's answers to the plan (2026-10-11):
- "dearer" means the day-ahead price;
- Finland is modelled as a zone on v5's engine;
- the cases are the owner's, Fingrid's two and the line between them.

Branch `research/finland-2035`. Scripts: `spikes/finland/`.

## 0. The rules, written before the results

- **The hypothesis is tested, not assumed.**
  - The evidence is reported both ways.
  - Fingrid's own forecast, Finland a net exporter of 10–16 TWh in 2035, stands beside the
    owner's case.
  - The answer is the line beyond which Finland is dearer than Estonia, not one number.
- **v5 stays as it is.**
  - Finland stays a boundary profile in the app (D2, RSK-8). The Finnish zone model lives in
    `spikes/finland/`.
  - Its series enters v5 only in the research's own runs.
  - v5's data scope stays D-002's. The Swedish and Norwegian series are read from the v4 cache
    with v4's lineage records, read-only; nothing is fetched.
- **Sources** are free and registered: documentary values with their pages, network data with
  their lineage.
- **The zone model is tested twice before any 2035 run.**
  - **Test 1, TYNDP's own Finland.** Given TYNDP 2024 DE 2035's inputs for each climate year, the
    model's Finnish price must meet TYNDP's FI00 marginal cost:
    - the mean within ±10 %;
    - Spearman ≥ 0.8;
    - the net export within ±10 %.
  - **Test 2, Finland's record, 2023–2025.** Given the observed load, the observed nuclear, hydro,
    wind and solar, and the neighbours' observed prices and offered capacities, the model's price
    must meet Finland's per year:
    - the mean within ±10 %;
    - P5 / P50 / P95 within ±15 % (or ±5 €/MWh where the value is under 33);
    - the hours at or below 5 and at or above 150 €/MWh within ±25 %;
    - Spearman ≥ 0.85;
    - the share of import-limited hours on each border within ±10 points;
    - the net flow on each border within ±20 % of the observed (A11).
    - Anything fitted is fitted on 2023–2024; 2025 is blind (as `R-VAL-1`).
  - **The stop rule:** a model that fails Test 2 on 2025 is not used for 2035, and the failure
    is reported.
- **The 2035 cases**, written before their runs. Each runs climate years 1995, 2008 and 2009 at
  v5's weights.
  - **A · Fingrid Moderate (Q3 2025):**
    - demand 104 TWh;
    - wind 53 TWh, solar 7 GW;
    - nuclear 35–36 TWh, other thermal 10 TWh;
    - Aurora Line 2 from 2034, no EstLink 3.
  - **B · Fingrid Excellent:** demand 159 TWh, wind 95 TWh (33 GW), solar 14 GW.
  - **C · The owner's case, the demand comes and the wind does not:**
    - data centres at the 3 GW with signed agreements, run at about 70 %;
    - the Arctial smelter, 7 TWh;
    - other demand as Moderate;
    - wind at the 2025–26 pace (about 15 GW), no offshore wind;
    - no Aurora Line 2 before 2035, no EstLink 3.
  - **D · The line:** data-centre load 0–4 GW against wind 12–33 GW, the smelter on and off. It
    finds where Finland's average crosses Estonia's.
- **Finland in v5.** Each case's series stands in FI's place in v5's opening view, per climate
  year.
  - EstLink couples the two: the zone model takes EstLink's flow from v5 as given, damped by half
    a step.
  - They alternate until both averages move by less than 0.5 €/MWh, at most six rounds (raised to
    eight after the first runs, §3a).
- **The record.** Annual average day-ahead prices, €/MWh, from the database:

| | 2016 | 2017 | 2018 | 2019 | 2020 | 2021 | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 | 2026 Jan–Sep |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| FI | 32.4 | 33.2 | 46.8 | 44.0 | 28.0 | 72.3 | 154.0 | 56.5 | 45.6 | 40.5 | 59.5 |
| EE | 33.1 | 33.2 | 47.1 | 45.9 | 33.7 | 86.7 | 192.8 | 90.8 | 87.3 | 80.4 | 80.1 |

  Until 2019 the two were one price within 2 €/MWh. The gap of 30–47 €/MWh opened from 2020.

## 1. The evidence, both ways (Phase 1)

Free sources, read 2026-10-11, each with its page. Network figures already in v5 are noted as such.
Values the research uses are named in §3.

**Demand.**
- **Consumption has no trend so far:** 82.5 TWh in 2015, 84.5 in 2025 (Statistics Finland 12sv;
  Fingrid 84.6). Fingrid's Q3 2025 forecast for 2035 is 104 (Moderate) or 159 TWh (Excellent),
  with industry and data centres at 44 or 96 (pp. 5, 7). It cut its forecasts between 2024 and 2025.
- **Data centres.**
  - Signed connection agreements: above 3 GW (Fingrid H1 2026), nearly 5 GW counting distribution
    connections (Fingrid, 18 Aug 2026). If all are built, consumption is about 40 % above 2025's, at
    least five years out.
  - Built or building:
    - Microsoft, Espoo 173 MW IT, Kirkkonummi ~150 MW and Vihti ~265 MW (EIA documents);
    - Nebius, Mäntsälä 75 + 70 MW and Lappeenranta 310 MW from 2027;
    - atNorth Kouvola, phase 1 60 MW;
    - Verne 70 MW;
    - Hyperco Kouvola 64 + 130 MW.
  - Google's €13 bn (2027–28: Hamina, Kajaani, Muhos, Vaala) has no MW published. LVV ordered the
    Kajaani and Muhos works halted in October 2026, pending their EIAs.
  - The industry's own figures:
    - built capacity runs at about 60 %;
    - use was 1.6 TWh in 2024, with 5–6 TWh expected in 2030 (the government's Mattila report, via
      FDCA).
  - Fingrid's view: about 10 % of the 50 GW of data-centre enquiries is realistic. Inflexible load is
    up only about 200 MW year on year into winter 2026–27.
- **Arctial** (Kokkola/Kronoby): 610 kt/a of aluminium.
  - Electricity: 8.1 TWh a year in the EIA programme and above 9 TWh in the largest variant of the
    EIA report (about 1 050 MW). The 7 TWh of Fortum's 2024 release is the older figure.
  - The EIA concluded on 8 October 2026. The investment decision is due Q2 2027, first metal H2 2029.
    There is a letter of intent with Fingrid, no connection agreement.
- **Electric boilers:** above 3 GW built or building. They made 2.6 TWh of heat in 2025 and averaged
  433 MW in winter 2025–26 (1 140 MW at most). They run in cheap hours, but 933 MW was on at the
  15 553 MW record peak (8 January 2026).
- **Hydrogen:** 20 MW operates (Harjavalta). Neste's 120 MW is cancelled and Flexens is bankrupt.
  The 500 MW Vetyalfa project reaches an investment decision around 2029 at the earliest.
- **Policy:**
  - Data centres moved to the full electricity tax from 1 July 2026: 0.05 → 2.24 c/kWh, Act
    1352/2025. The planned refund was dropped in September 2026.
  - A 1 250 m setback for wind turbines (HE 70/2026) is in Parliament, for 1 January 2027.

**Supply.**
- **Onshore wind:** 9 434 MW at the end of 2025 and 9 535 MW at mid-2026.
  - Built per year: +2 430 MW (2022), +1 280 (2023), +1 465 (2024), +1 023 (2025), +106 (H1 2026).
  - 0.82–0.90 GW is under construction, finishing by 2028; 3.7 GW is permitted.
  - The pipeline shrank from 56.3 to 52.6 GW in six months (Renewables Finland).
  - WindEurope expects about 12 GW in 2030.
  - The reasons given are cheap power (Energiateollisuus), slow demand (WindEurope, Fingrid) and
    Swedish imports after Aurora (Fingrid).
- **Offshore wind:**
  - Finland has 71 MW. Of 16 GW in territorial waters, nothing is permitted or under construction;
    the EEZ tender keeps slipping.
  - In the region, tenders without a two-sided CfD failed: Denmark 2024, Germany 2025, Lithuania
    2025, and Estonia's support was dropped. Denmark 2026 and Poland 2025 succeeded with CfDs.
    Finland has none.
- **Solar:** about 1.6 GW at the end of 2025 (Energy Authority); utility-scale reached 842 MW by
  mid-2026. Fingrid's 2035 is 7 or 14 GW.
- **Nuclear:** 4.4 GW.
  - Loviisa runs to 2050 with +38 MW; Google's PPA takes up to half of it from 2030.
  - OL1/2 are licensed to 2038, an uprate of +160 MW is possible, and OL2 is derated to 735 MW until
    2027.
  - Fingrid's 2035: 35–36 TWh.
- **CHP and reserves:** coal ended in 2025, Meri-Pori closes in March 2027, CHP is about 2.5 GW, and
  the strategic reserve is 0 MW for three seasons. Fingrid's thermal output falls from ≈13 to
  ≈10 TWh by 2035, and it asks for 750–1 000 MW of new flexible capacity by 2030.
- **Batteries:** about 1.6 GW installed, ~1 GW under construction, ~4 GW with grid agreements.

**Cables.**
- Aurora Line, since 13 November 2025: SE1↔FI from 1 200/1 100 MW to 1 900 MW each way.
- Aurora Line 2: about +1 000 MW, assumed for 2034 by Fingrid, no investment decision.
- Fenno-Skan: 1 200 MW; Fenno-Skan 3 around 2038.
- EstLink 1+2: 1 016 MW. EstLink 3 (700 MW) has slipped to 2038.
- NO4: about 120/100 MW. Russia: 0.
- Fingrid's 1-in-10 cold calm day: 15 GW of demand, 11.7–12 GW domestic, 3.2–3.3 GW of imports
  against about 4.1 GW of capacity. Adequacy weakens around 2030.

**Sweden north**, Finland's import side (Svenska kraftnät).
- KMA 2025 (March 2026), 2026→2030:
  - SE1 25 → 45 €/MWh;
  - Finland 33 → 41;
  - Estonia 52 → 48, about 6–19 above Finland every year.
- LMA 2024's 2035: Finland 40.9 / 58.8 / 43.9 / 46.1, SE1 40.5–63.6.
- LMA 2026's 2040: Finland 66.8–73.7, SE1 55–81, Lithuania 80–91.
- SE1's demand rests on projects that slip:
  - Stegra: no start date, more capital needed;
  - LKAB's Kiruna switch: moved to the 2040s.

**Prices.**
- **The record**, Estonia minus Finland:
  - 0–2 €/MWh in 2017–2019;
  - 34–42 in 2023–2025;
  - 20 in 2026 to 12 October (79.0 against 58.7); Estonia is dearer in every month of 2026.
- **Forwards**, Euronext, 9 October 2026:
  - Finland (system + Helsinki EPAD): 61.5 / 57.0 / 55.1 / 55.1 for 2027–2030;
  - SE1 37–39;
  - Vilnius EPAD implies Lithuania at 104 / 89 / 82 for 2027–2029, from very few trades.
  - No Estonian forward exists: there is no Tallinn EPAD, and EEX lists no Baltic product
    (Konkurentsiamet).
- **The one study that models both:** Ea Energy Analyses for Estonia's Ministry of Climate (March
  2026, Balmorel; 2022 euros).
  - In SC2, Estonia and Finland are about level in 2035, at ≈54; Finland is the region's dearest
    from 2040, 2–4 €/MWh above Estonia.
  - Without a new EE–FI cable (SC2_2), Finland is above Estonia from 2035 and power flows from
    Estonia to Finland.
  - Its modelled 2026 Baltic price (64) sits 16–20 below 2025's record.
- **AFRY 2025:** strong data-centre growth (2.5 GW) raises Finland's average by about 10 % within
  five years.

**Beside the day-ahead price.** The owner's 2023 item has reversed:
- Fingrid's balance volume fee went 1.20 → 1.50 €/MWh in November 2023, then 1.73 in 2025, and down
  to 0.40 by July 2026 and 0.00 for October–November 2026. Reserves cost less than forecast.
- Finland's reserves cost ≈2.2–2.6 €/MWh of consumption; Estonia's ≈8.9 in 2025 after
  synchronisation.
- Estonia also adds a balancing-capacity fee of 3.73, a security-of-supply fee of 7.58 and a
  renewables fee of 8.40 €/MWh. Its 110 kV tariff doubled in May 2026.
- Eurostat 2025, a 20–70 GWh industrial user: network 20.9 + levies 10.2 €/MWh in Estonia, against
  13.4 + 0.6 in Finland.
- An inversion of the day-ahead price by a few euros would not invert the all-in price.

## 2. The Finnish zone model (`spikes/finland/`)

**What it is.**
- `zone.ts`: one zone cleared by v5's `runLP` on a Problem built by hand. It checks its own hourly
  balance (< 1e-5 MW).
- `prep.py`: the record's inputs, hour by hour in UTC.
  - Finland's load, generation by type and price, Estonia's price and EstLink: v5's database.
  - SE1, SE3 and NO4: the v4 cache, each file checked against its v4 lineage sha256.
  - The Swedish borders' capacities: A31 to 2024-10-28; from the Nordic flow-based go-live, JAO's
    maximum bilateral exchanges. These are an upper bound per border, not a simultaneously
    feasible set.
  - NO4 enters as its observed flow: its JAO maximum is SE1's, the same constraint.
- `tests.ts`: Test 2. In the replay:
  - Thermal plant runs the week's 10th-percentile hour as heat-bound output. Above it, up to the
    week's maximum, it bids on a ladder read from the record of the fit years: the share of that
    range run at each price.
  - Hydro releases the week's observed energy between the week's 10th and 90th percentile hours
    (D-044's rule).
  - Wind and solar are offered at 0.
  - Each neighbour is a price-taker at its observed price.

**A gap in the data, corrected before the test.** ENTSO-E's Finnish generation plus net imports falls
short of the load:
- 2023: by 150–620 MW a month, most in winter;
- 2024: January–May.

From June 2024 the record closes within ±100 MW. A month short by more than 100 MW gets the shortfall
as given output, shaped like its thermal output, which is plant ENTSO-E does not report. The blind
year 2025 carries no correction.

## 2a. Test 2's results

The ladder fitted on 2023–2024: 23 % of the range runs at any price, then 7 % above 40 €/MWh, 10 %
above 60, 4 % above 80, 12 % above 100, 18 % above 150, 4 % above 300, and 22 % above 1 000.

| | Mean | P5 / P50 / P95 | h ≤ 5 | h ≥ 150 | Spearman | Price criteria |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2023 (fit) | 53.6 / 56.5 | 0 / 40.0 / 149.6 \| 0 / 42.8 / 153.8 | 1 343 / 1 327 | 414 / 487 | 0.952 | all pass |
| 2024 (fit) | 44.3 / 45.6 | 0 / 25.2 / 132.9 \| −0.6 / 27.6 / 143.4 | 2 289 / 2 203 | 325 / 381 | 0.929 | all pass |
| **2025 (blind)** | **51.0 / 40.5** | 0 / **32.9** / 150.0 \| 0 / **16.7** / 144.1 | **2 157 / 2 900** | 453 / 388 | 0.861 | **mean, P50, h ≤ 5 fail** |

Model / record throughout.

**Border criteria.**
- **SE1 fails in every year.** Its "import-limited hours" are 49–78 % in the model against 3–16 % in
  the record, and its net flow is 6–8 TWh against 4–6.
  - SE1-FI is an AC border. In the hours Finland is dearer than SE1, when the market's exchange is
    at its limit, the physical flow (A11) runs at a median of 80–87 % of the capacity.
  - The criterion compares the model's commercial exchange with a physical flow. For this border it
    measures the wrong thing.
- **SE3 (HVDC) and EstLink** pass the import-limited criterion in 2023–2024. SE3's net flow is 2.0
  TWh against 2.5–3.4.

**Test 2 as written fails: the blind year misses its mean, median and cheap hours.** The miss is in
spring 2025: April 89 against 48 €/MWh, May 41 against 18, June 36 against 19. Its cause is in the
borders under flow-based:
- JAO's maximum SE1→FI exchange averaged 226 MW in June 2025, while the physical flow ran at 700 MW
  and more.
- The maximum FI→SE3 exchange (about 1 000 MW) lets the model sell to SE3 at SE3's price and pulls
  Finland's price up to it.
- In the record, Finland stayed with SE1 (2.6 against 2.4 €/MWh in the cheapest hours) while
  importing from SE3. These are flow-based's "non-intuitive" flows, which no border-by-border
  capacity reproduces.
- **The miss is one-sided:** +10.5 €/MWh on Finland's average. It favours the owner's hypothesis.

**Added after the run: a hold-out inside the capacity era.** The ladder is fitted on 2023 alone, and
2024 up to 28 October is blind.

| | Mean | P5 / P50 / P95 | h ≤ 5 | h ≥ 150 | Spearman |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2024-01-01…10-28 | 45.3 / 46.5 | 0 / 25.6 / 129.1 \| −0.8 / 28.5 / 140.8 | 1 782 / 1 713 | 254 / 298 | 0.926 |

Every price criterion passes. SE3's and EstLink's border criteria pass; SE1's fails as above. The
model's economics hold where the borders are given as capacities, which is how the 2035 inputs of this
research give them: TYNDP's reference grid and Fingrid's stated capacities.

**Test 1 is deferred, a departure from §0.** `tyndp.py` extracts TYNDP DE 2035's Finland. For climate
year 2009:
- Finland 44.9 €/MWh;
- SE1 47.8, SE3 50.2, EE 54.2;
- capacities SE1 4 271 / 4 371 MW (Aurora Lines 1 and 2 and a concept), SE3 1 700, EE 1 703
  (EstLink 3's 687 MW included).

The Finnish node is split:
- an electrolysis node (FI00RETE) takes 29.6 TWh;
- dedicated-renewables hydrogen nodes (FI00 DRES, FI00 SRES) and hydrogen zones set the
  electrolysers' hourly bids.

Reproducing Finland's price there means reproducing TYNDP's hydrogen market, and none of the 2035
cases carries one (the last log's V2). Test 2, on the record, is the binding test.

**The owner's decision (2026-10-11), after Test 2.** "Continue with the correction": the model is used for
2035 with the borders given as capacities. Each case is also shown with the flow-based shift: the
blind year's error removed quantile by quantile, i.e. 2025's record minus the model's 2025, at the
same rank. The hypothesis-favouring error then stays visible.

## 3a. The first runs, as defined: Finland without enough capacity

`v5.ts`, the three cases × v5's four scenarios. Climate years are weighted; FI is the zone model's
average, EE v5's.

**What this shows.**
- **B and C leave Finland short of capacity.** On a cold, calm hour the case's demand reaches about
  18–19 GW:
  - TYNDP's shape peaks at 1.36 times its average;
  - plus the data centres and the smelter.
- Against it stand about 15 GW: nuclear 4.4, hydro ~2.5, CHP ~2.2, batteries 2 and imports ~4.1.
- The averages are then set by load shed at the price cap, 4 000 €/MWh. They are not a price
  forecast.
- This is Fingrid's own warning. It asks for 750–1 000 MW of new flexible capacity by 2030 for
  today's demand, and the ministry prepares capacity support from 2027.
- **Even A has a few dozen hours at the cap.** These hours make the coupling with v5 swing: Finland's
  average moves 10–20 €/MWh from round to round while EstLink's average flow holds at about
  −600 MW. None of the runs converged within six rounds.

**The adequacy response, added after these runs.** The market and Fingrid answer a shortage before
2035; the cases are rerun with that answer.
- **The new large loads' flexibility:** 30 % of the data centres' and the smelter's load is offered
  as demand response at 500 €/MWh. This follows Fingrid's rule since 12 June 2026: a new load of
  30 MW or more must be able to cut 30 %.
- **New peakers:** gas turbines at v5's own bid for `gas_ocgt` at the scenario's fuels.
  - They are added in steps of 250 MW until Finland's hours of unserved load, over the three
    climate years weighted, are at most 2.1 a year: Finland's reliability standard (Fingrid).
  - The sizing gives Finland EstLink as a border at Estonia's price from v5's first run, 1 016 MW
    each way.
- How many peakers each case needs is a result in its own right.

## 6. What the research finds (the gate)

**The hypothesis**, Finland's day-ahead price above Estonia's by 2035, holds only where three things
meet:
- the demand comes: data centres at 2–4 GW of average load on top of Arctial;
- the wind does not: about 15 GW, the pace of 2025–26;
- Estonia is long: three SMR units, or two with the data centres at their very top.

**Under Fingrid's own forecasts** (A, B) and under the owner's case (C), Finland stays cheaper
without the plant: by 25–29 €/MWh (A, B) and by 18.6 (C).

**Finland's tightness raises Estonia's price too.** In the owner's case Estonia gains 19 €/MWh over
Fingrid Moderate. Through that, the plant's figures move:
- On 2 SMR the capture price is 94.4 €/MWh and the hourly CfD pays the state 22 M€ a year, against
  68.2 and a cost of 110 in force.
- With three units, Finland and Estonia meet at about 82 €/MWh.

**The cases stand on new firm capacity.** The owner's case needs 2.75 GW of new peakers in Finland
(Fingrid Excellent 4 GW) to keep the reliability standard. Without it, the result is load shedding
and prices at the cap (§3a). Fingrid already asks for 0.75–1 GW by 2030.

**The evidence beside it.**
- **Against inversion before 2030:** the record (Estonia above Finland in every month of 2026), the
  forwards (Finland 55–62, Lithuania 82–104), and SvK's KMA (Estonia 6–19 above to 2030).
- **For inversion in the 2030s:** Ea Energy for Estonia's ministry (level in 2035, Finland above
  without EstLink 3, and from 2040).
- **Of the owner's four drivers:**
  - Offshore wind failing is right, and it removes offshore wind from Finland's 2035.
  - Google is real, but no MW is published and two sites are halted.
  - Arctial is now 8–9 TWh, its investment decision due 2027.
  - Fingrid's balance fee fell to 0 in October 2026, while Estonia's reserves cost about 9 €/MWh.
- **All-in, Estonia stays dearer** for an industrial user by 20–30 €/MWh of levies and network
  charges, whatever the day-ahead price does.

**The model's limits.**
- It prices the blind flow-based year 10.5 €/MWh too high for Finland, in the hypothesis's favour;
  the shifted columns show the size.
- The Swedish neighbours are profiles.
- Test 1 (TYNDP's own Finland) is deferred.
- The coupling's last rounds move the averages by about 1 €/MWh.
