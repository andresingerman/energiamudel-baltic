# OPEN-2: a correction of PECD's onshore wind capacity factor, measured

*2026-10-08 · executor (the night run) · on branch `night/2026-10-09`, after D-105 and D-106 ·
corrected after the night's two reviews (the vintage option's fleet; the reading of the record) ·
for the owner's decision; nothing in the model changes.*

## The question

Scenario years run on PECD 3.1's capacity factors (target year 2030); observed years on the
record's. Methodology §2 (R-TIME-5) measures the observed fleet's onshore wind factor against
PECD's climatology: Lithuania 0.61 (2020–2025; 0.67 on the same years 2015–2019), Latvia 0.83,
Estonia 0.98. OPEN-2's default is no correction, to be decided "at the end of M3"; the deadline
has passed, and D-074 noted that Lithuania at 0.80 of PECD raises its opening price by about 5
€/MWh.

## What the record says

Lithuania's onshore fleet (ENTSO-E A68, 1 January) grew from 534 MW in 2020 to 540, 671, 814,
1 288, 1 683 and 2 507 MW in 2026; its yearly capacity factor by §2's method (A75 over the
average of the year's two 1 January capacities) was 0.319, 0.236, 0.227, 0.261, 0.252 and 0.209
in 2020–2025. Turbines built since 2020 make 1, 12, 28, 49, 64 and 75 % of those years'
capacity — 38 % on the plain average of years §2 takes, 51 % of the MW-years. The yearly factors
are not weather-corrected (Estonia's flat fleet went from 0.305 to 0.243 in 2020–2022), so the
series alone cannot show whether new turbines do better; but 2023 and 2024, with 49–64 % of the
fleet new, gave 0.261 and 0.252 against PECD's 0.410, and the older fleet's ratio on the same
years 2015–2019 was 0.67, above the recent 0.61. Nothing in the record shows the new fleet near
PECD's factor.

Two effects may push the measured ratio down, by amounts the cache cannot give:

- A75 counts output after curtailment, and the engine curtails on its own; a factor taken from
  A75 counts curtailment twice.
- The average of two 1 January capacities overstates the capacity in service if a year's
  additions came late in it (2025: 1 683 → 2 507 MW); spread evenly through the year they would
  average out.

So 0.61 is, if anything, low for Lithuania, and PECD's 1.0 is not supported by the record. Latvia's
0.83 rests on a fleet of 79–151 MW over the measured years.

Baltic south's rise. Estonia's ratio, 0.98, is left alone in every option.

## Reading

- The vintage split is an assumption, not the record's finding; its factor, 0.823, happens to land
  beside the round 0.80.
- The record's ratio on the whole fleet takes the measurement at face value; with the two effects
  above it is more likely too low than too high, but neither is measured.
- Lithuania at 0.80 is a round judgement between the measured ratio and PECD, not a point the
  evidence fixes; it moves the opening state by +3.8 €/MWh in Estonia and +6.0 in Latvia and
  Lithuania.

## Recommendation

Adopt Lithuania's onshore wind at 0.80 of PECD's capacity factor in scenario years as an interim
answer to OPEN-2 — a factor on the capacity factor, not on the megawatts (the slow-build cap of
R-SUP-9 reads megawatts), with its lineage to this note and §2 — leave Latvia and Estonia
uncorrected, re-run the methodology's scenario figures, and replace the 0.80 when a measurement
with curtailment added back and capacity by commissioning month is made. The replays stay as they
are (they use observed factors). If the owner prefers to keep the default until then, quote this
table in methodology §2.
