# v3 and v5 on the same years: which predicts the Baltic price better (2026-10-18)

**Why.** The owner asked which model is better, then for their accuracy on the same years: "make a comparison of v3 and v5 by accuracy on the same years".

**Answer.** v5. On their own published backtests v3 looks better in 2024–2025, but the two backtests
do not measure the same thing.
- **v3's backtest reads the observed price.** Its replay of a past year anchors each neighbour's supply
  on that hour's observed Baltic price and observed cross-border flow. It also takes Kruonis's and
  the Daugava's observed hourly output.
- **v5's backtest reads no observed Baltic price** (SPECIFICATION O-3, R-BND-3).
- **On equal terms v3 is two to three times worse.** Run without the anchor, v3's hourly error is
  26–32 €/MWh in 2023–2025, against v5's 8–18.

## The two backtests

v3 3.5.0 is the version archived at v3.energiamudel.ee; its repository was read only. Its validator,
`pipeline/validate_fit.mjs`, runs the default scenario on every committed history year. Its baseline was
frozen on 2026-07-20.

In a replay, v3 takes these from the year's record (`app/src/coupler.js`, `app/src/engine.js`):

| What | How | Where in v3 |
|---|---|---|
| Each neighbour's supply | `export = fHist(h) + 20 MW/€ × (p − pAnchor(h))`. Here `fHist` is the link's observed flow and `pAnchor` the attached Baltic zone's **observed price** that hour. The code's own comment: "when the scenario reproduces history (p ≈ node's historical price) the link carries its historical flow" | `neighbourExport`, coupler.js 222–259 |
| Kruonis and the Daugava | The year's actual hourly output, injected as must-run (`REAL_MUSTRUN`). Optimising Kruonis is an option, off by default | engine.js 29–30; coupler.js 133–139; state.js 97 |
| Gas CHP | A floor at a monthly quantile of observed output | coupler.js 90–112 |

**v3 was also calibrated on 2025.** Its ADR-004 makes 2025 "the default base year and the calibration
target": its bids were tuned so that the modelled 2025 prices and CO₂ "land within a few percent of the
observed year" (v3 `ADR.md` 54–66, `sources.md` 70–71). So 2025 is in-sample for v3. For v5, 2025 is
the blind year: its parameters are fitted on 2023–2024 (§7.2).

v5's replay takes the neighbours' **own** observed prices at the far end of each cable and the
published capacities. It takes no Baltic price and no flow. From the record it also takes:
- weekly bounds on the thermal plants;
- the Daugava's weekly band and budget;
- Kruonis's weekly power and energy.

The Baltic price, the flows and the hourly dispatch are the model's own (methodology §7.1).

**The observed prices are the same series.** Both are ENTSO-E day-ahead prices with equal annual
means; v3 holds them in local time (EET/EEST), v5 in UTC (the best alignment is at −2 h, −3 h in
summer). Each model is scored against its own series, aligned with its own inputs.

## Results

v5's metrics (`app/src/worker/metrics.ts`) on both models. v3 is run three ways
(`spikes/v3-comparison/accuracy.ts`, which writes `accuracy.json`; from `app/`):
- **v3 as published:** its own replay. The numbers reproduce its committed baseline exactly.
- **v3 without the anchor:** each neighbour supplies around its own price, `20 MW/€ × (p − pNeighbour)`,
  with no observed flow. That is v3's formulation before its audit A3.
- **v3 blind:** without the anchor, and with Kruonis scheduled by v3's own storage option instead of
  its observed output.

## What it shows

1. **v3's published fit is largely its anchor.** With the observed price taken out of the neighbours'
   supply, v3's error roughly triples: from 8–12 to 26–32 €/MWh. Its duration-curve error doubles.
   The anchor is a sound design for v3's purpose, to show a scenario as a deviation from a real year.
   It is not a test of how well the model forms prices.
2. **On equal terms v5 is two to three times more accurate.** v5's hourly error is 8–18 €/MWh and its
   duration-curve error 3–9, against v3's 21–33 without the anchor.
   - Even in 2025, v5's weakest year, v5's 17–18 is well below v3's 27–32.
   - On hourly error and duration-curve error, in every year and zone, v5 beats v3 without the
     anchor. On the year's average price, v3 without the anchor is closer in 2024, by 0.8–1.4 €/MWh;
     v5 is closer in 2023 and 2025.
3. **v3's anchor even beats v5 on 2024–2025 hours.** v3 as published is closer to the record than v5
   in 2024 for Latvia and Lithuania, and in 2025 everywhere. That is what an input that already holds
   the answer does, and 2025 was also the year v3 was calibrated on. It also shows how much v5's 2025
   error leaves to gain.
4. **Where fuel prices swung, v5's fuel-based bids are the difference.** In 2020–2022 v3's static bids
   miss by 28–56 €/MWh even with the anchor; v5 misses by 14–22 without one.

## Limits of the comparison

- **v3 was tuned with its anchor.** "Without the anchor" changes v3's neighbour model and keeps its
  other parameters (bids, CHP floor, battery cost), which were tuned under the anchored replay.
  Re-tuned for a blind replay, v3 would do better than shown. How much is not measured; closing a
  gap of two to three times would take a different model.
- **v3 keeps an advantage in every variant.** The Daugava's observed hourly output stays in, as v5
  takes only its weekly band.
- **Kruonis.** "v3 blind" is worse than "without the anchor": v3's storage option schedules Kruonis
  worse than its observed output does.
- **v5's own limits stand.** v5's 2025 error is above its own target of 15 (§7.3; accepted for
  release, D-123). Its average price runs a few euros low (§10).
