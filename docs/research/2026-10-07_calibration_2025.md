## 2. Where the 2025 error sits

**By month.** Estonia's monthly MAE runs 11, 46, 26, 19, 13, 5, 8, 11, 16, 32, 31, 19 €/MWh
(January to December); Latvia's and Lithuania's 11, 43, 28, 17, 13, 5, 6, 9, 13, 29, 25, 16.
February, October and November — a quarter of the hours — carry 45 / 44 / 45 % of the year's
absolute error. Without February the year stands at 17.43 / 15.57 / 15.38; without February,
October and November at 14.34 / 12.99 / 12.75. (A diagnostic, not a proposal to drop months.)

**By price level.** Hours the record priced above 150 €/MWh (Estonia: 981 at 150–250, 240 above
250) hold 42 % of Estonia's error; the model is 22 €/MWh low in the first bin and 127 low in the
second (2024: −22 and −57; 2023: −10 and −58). Below 60 €/MWh it is 6–11 high. The model's
price never passes 467 €/MWh; the record reached 1 000. The price distribution is compressed at
both ends.

**By hour of day.** Latvia's bias by UTC hour: +3…+6 at night, −9…−10 at 04–05 (the morning
ramp), +1…+3 at midday, −6…−11 at 15–18 (the evening peak); Estonia's is the same, deeper in the
evening (−15, −16). The model's day is too flat.

**At the borders, in the dear hours.** In the 981 Estonian hours at 150–250 €/MWh the model
clears Estonia at Finland's price in 263 hours, the record in 198; Lithuania at SE4's in 291
against 223. Above 250: 69 against 36 and 67 against 38. EstLink ran at its offered capacity in
750 and 195 of those hours. Where the market was short and the cables full, the model still finds
supply cheaper than the neighbours'.

**Which supply.** In the hours Latvia's record priced above 250 €/MWh Kruonis delivers 229 MW in
the model against 142 in the record (2024: 337 against 224; 2023: 258 against 180); the Daugava
releases 454 and 499 MW in the 100–150 and 150–250 bins against 380 and 446, and 195 against 270
in the hours below 30. In Estonia's dear hours the oil shale runs 410 MW against 349. The weekly
energies are the record's (D-018, D-044); what differs is where in the week the model puts them —
with perfect foresight, in the dearest hours.

**The worst days.** 26–28 February (model 313 / 261 / 258 against 164 / 196 / 193: the markup's
top tranches where the market priced lower), 15 February (147 against 266), 31 March (Latvia and
Lithuania 122 against 305), 20 October, 3 November (Estonia 81 against 184; 105–129 against
159–507 in the day's hours, with Latvia's gas at 450–480 MW in the record, Lithuania's at 56–77,
and the model running 100 MW more oil shale than the record), 25 and 27 November, 1 December.

## 3. A data error found: NordBalt open while it was out (ERRATA E-073, D-091 proposed)

ENTSO-E publishes no offered capacity for NordBalt in 47 hours of 30 March – 1 April 2025, and
the export filled them with the week before's 700 MW (D-013). A forced outage at 0 MW is published
for 29 March 06:30 – 2 April 13:20 and the scheduled exchange is 0 throughout. The fix bounds a
filled hour by a published forced outage; planned outages are left out — NordBalt carried 448 MW
on 10 September 2025 inside a week published as planned at 0 MW, and capping by planned records
made 2025 worse by 0.47. With forced outages only, 2025 moves to 19.60 / 17.66 / 17.46 (blind
54.71) and nothing else changes: on 31 March the model gains (−31 €/MWh summed over the three
zones' daily error), but the closed cable moves the week's storage plan and costs the days before.
The fix is for the record's sake.

## 5a. A scarcity function in place of the bins, and a flat markup

The plan asked for the markup's form as a scarcity function of the margin *in place of* the four
bins; §4's adder sat on top of them. With the markup at zero and the Latvian and Lithuanian gas
bidding a·((T − T₀)/(1 − T₀))ᵏ over its cost — T the Baltic hour's residual demand over its thermal,
import and storage capacity, from the replay's own inputs — the fit years' best point lies at the
grid's gentle edge, a 60 €/MWh, T₀ 0, k 0.25: a nearly flat uplift. So the form tells what a flat
markup tells. Scored as the protocol scores (register after D-094: fit 62.82, blind 54.71):

| Form | Fit 2023–2024, in sample | Each fit year alone, scored on the other | Blind 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|
| The four bins (the register, 40 / 195 / 315 / 335) | 62.82 | 63.62 | 54.71 (19.59 / 17.66 / 17.46) |
| Flat 60 €/MWh | 65.89 | 65.88 (each year chooses 60) | 52.18 (18.58 / 16.90 / 16.70) |
| Scarcity function, a 60, T₀ 0, k 0.25 | 66.07 | 66.07 (each year chooses it) | 52.25 (18.61 / 16.92 / 16.72) |

By every criterion the fit years offer — in sample and each year scored on the other — the bins
win, by 3.1 and 2.3; the protocol keeps them, and nothing is proposed. The blind year reverses the
order by 2.5: the bins' steep upper tranches, which the fit years do not pin down (§5), price
2025's tight hours above the market (26–28 February, §2), where a flatter markup does not. Taking
the flat form because of that would spend the blind year (R-VAL-1). It is the night's clearest
sign that 2025 is a regime the fit years do not contain, and it belongs to §6.1's question; no
form reaches 15 in 2025 (the best, 18.58 / 16.90 / 16.70).

## 6. What the gate would need

No lever the fit years can identify moves the blind year by more than about 0.2 of the summed
MAE; the gate cannot be met honestly with this model class and this protocol. What would:

1. **A model of the dear hours that 2023–2024 can teach.** The fit years hold 104–106 (2023) and
   218–219 (2024) hours at or above 250 €/MWh against 245–252 in 2025; the markup's upper tranches are
   not pinned down by them (§5), and a flatter markup the fit years reject scores 2.5 better on the
   blind year (§5a). A scarcity or bidding component needs fit data that has such hours — which
   means moving the protocol's windows (for instance fitting on 2024 and the first half of 2025,
   scoring the second half and 2026), the owner's decision.
2. **Storage and hydro without perfect foresight.** Kruonis and the Daugava place the record's
   weekly energy in the week's dearest hours; the market did not (§2). A rolling horizon or a
   forecast error in the weekly programme, or bidding rules for the stores, would restore the
   day's shape. An engine change, the owner's to ask for.
3. **The two regimes of 2025.** February: desynchronisation from the Russian system (8–9
   February), EstLink 2 out to June, the balancing-capacity market from February — one month
   carrying 18–19 % of the year's error. October–November: Estonia between full borders with its
   oil shale setting the price above the entry prices of R-SUP-4 (the note of 2026-10-05); Eesti
   Energia's oil-shale and other fossil units produced 57 GWh in Q3 2025, down 83 % year on year,
   "the Auvere power plant underwent a major overhaul and market conditions limited dispatchable
   generation due to low market prices" ([Eesti Energia, Q3 2025 results, 7 Nov
   2025](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2025/11/07/3183373/0/en/Eesti-Energia-Group-Unaudited-Results-for-Q3-2025.html));
   from 1 January 2026 Elering pays up to €59.5 m a year for up to 1 036 MW of reserve
   capability, the units entitled to take part in other markets with the same capacity
   ([Eesti Energia, 11 Nov 2025](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2025/11/11/3185550/0/en/Eesti-Energia-Subsidiary-Enefit-Power-Wins-Tender-for-Reserve-Capability.html)).
   No document gives the units' market-entry price in either regime; without one, a parameter
   for it would be fitted to 2025 itself. The months of 2026 the export holds (January–August,
   outside both the fit and the blind year) point the same way: the model's
   Estonian price 11.0 €/MWh below the record on average and its oil shale 1.21 TWh against 0.79
   observed (2025: 1.74 against 1.42) — in the model the units run more than they did, on their
   costs and on a floor fitted on years in which they ran more (methodology §7.2).
4. **Treating February 2025 as a diagnostic period**, as 2022 is (R-VAL-7), is the owner's to
   decide; it would not meet the gate on its own (17.43 / 15.57 / 15.38 without it).
